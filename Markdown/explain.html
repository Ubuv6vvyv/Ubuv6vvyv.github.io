Setting Description Effect on Sound
Punch Controls the intensity of the initial sound impact. Increases the sharpness and power of the attack.
Tail Length Adjusts the duration of the sound after the initial hit. Longer tails produce sustained booms; shorter tails fade quickly.
Pitch Decay Changes how quickly the pitch drops after the attack. Fast decay creates a quick drop in pitch, while slow decay creates a longer, more dramatic pitch change.
Octave Range Sets the range of pitch modulation across octaves. Wider ranges create more dynamic and noticeable pitch shifts.
Attack Time Controls how fast the sound reaches its peak volume. Short attack times create a punchy sound; long attack times create a gradual build-up.
Decay Time Determines how long it takes for the sound to decrease after the attack. Short decay creates quick fades; long decay sustains the sound longer before fading.
Sustain Level Sets the volume level during the sustain phase after the decay. Higher levels keep the sound loud; lower levels reduce the volume after the initial hit.
Release Time Controls how long the sound takes to fade out after the note is released. Short release times stop the sound quickly; long release times let it fade out gradually.
Base Frequency Sets the starting pitch of the bass sound. Lower base frequencies create deeper, more resonant bass sounds.
Reverse Amount Adds a backward playback effect to the sound. Higher values create more pronounced reverse effects, adding uniqueness.
Filter Cutoff Determines the frequency point where the filter begins cutting off sounds. Lower cutoff frequencies remove more of the higher pitches.
Filter Resonance Emphasizes the frequencies around the cutoff point. Higher resonance creates a peak that makes the filtered sound more noticeable and sharp.
Filter Envelope Amount Adjusts the influence of the envelope on the filter's behavior over time. Greater amounts lead to more dynamic filter sweeps across the sound.
Oscillator Mix Blends different waveforms to create the final sound. Mixing various waveforms adds complexity and texture to the sound.
Sub Oscillator Level Adds an additional, deeper layer of sound underneath the main oscillator. Increases the depth and richness of the bass sound.
Kick Pitch Adjusts the overall pitch of the kick drum sound. Higher pitches result in a lighter kick; lower pitches produce a deeper kick.
Kick Tone Changes the texture and character of the kick drum sound. Different tones can make the kick sound smooth or rough, soft or aggressive.
Bass Pitch Sets the pitch level for the bass sound. Higher bass pitches make it sound brighter, lower pitches make it sound deeper.
Bass Tone Modifies the texture and quality of the bass sound. Various tones can make the bass sound clean, distorted, soft, or harsh.
Distortion Adds a layer of grit and roughness to the sound. More distortion makes the sound harsher and more aggressive.
Compression Balances the dynamic range of the sound, making quiet parts louder and loud parts quieter. Compression smooths out volume fluctuations, creating a more consistent sound level.
I Synthesizer Settings Guide

Synthesizer Settings Guide for Hardstyle and Hard Techno

Setting Description Effect on Sound When to Use Example
Attack Time Controls how quickly a sound hits its full volume after you press a key. A fast attack gives sharp, punchy sounds, while a slow attack creates a smooth swell. Use quick attack for hardstyle kicks. Use slow attack for atmospheric pads in hard techno. A hardstyle kick hits immediately; a rising pad in techno swells smoothly.
Base Frequency Sets the starting pitch of your sound. A lower base frequency gives a deep, sub-bass feel, while a higher base frequency makes it brighter. For bass-heavy kicks in hardstyle, keep base frequency low. For higher leads in hard techno, shift it higher. A low-pitched hardstyle kick creates a deep rumble; a higher-pitched lead in techno is sharp.
Bass Pitch Controls the pitch of the bass sound. Lower bass pitch makes the sound growl, while higher pitch adds definition. Use low pitch for hardstyle bass kicks. Use higher pitch for basslines in hard techno. A low-pitched kick in hardstyle booms; a higher bassline in techno adds energy.
Bass Tone Adjusts the character or color of the bass. Brighter tone gives sharpness; darker tone adds depth and body. Use bright tone for hardstyle kicks. Use dark tone for hard techno basslines. Bright bass in hardstyle for aggression; dark bass in techno for weight.
Compression Controls the loudness and dynamics, reducing the difference between loud and quiet parts. Compressing makes sounds more consistent, tightening bass and kicks for punch. Use compression in hardstyle for tight kicks. For hard techno, apply it to drums and bass. Compressed hardstyle kick punches through; compressed techno drums are tight.
Decay Time Controls how quickly the sound fades from its peak level to its sustain level. A short decay gives a snappy fade; a long decay keeps the sound alive. For hardstyle kicks, use short decay. For atmospheric or evolving sounds in techno, use longer decay. A short decay on a hardstyle kick gives sharpness; a long decay on a pad in techno creates smoothness.
Distortion Adds harshness by overdriving the sound, creating harmonic overtones. Distortion adds aggression and punch, making basslines and kicks stand out. Use distortion in hardstyle for aggressive kicks and leads. In hard techno, apply it to basslines and drums. Distorted hardstyle kick is louder and punchier; distorted techno bassline adds grit.
Filter Cutoff Controls where the filter cuts off high frequencies. A low cutoff makes sounds darker, while a high cutoff keeps things bright. Use a low cutoff for hardstyle bass and kicks. For techno, raise the cutoff for brighter leads. A low cutoff on a hardstyle kick makes it deep; a high cutoff on a techno lead keeps it sharp.
Filter Envelope Amount Controls how much the filter is affected by the envelope. More envelope amount opens or closes the filter during the sound, creating movement. Increase for dynamic, evolving leads and bass in hardstyle. In hard techno, use it on evolving pads. A hardstyle lead with filter envelope sounds wobbly; techno pads evolve smoothly.
Filter Resonance Boosts the frequencies around the cutoff point. Higher resonance gives a sharper, squelchy character to the sound. Use high resonance for hardstyle leads. For techno, moderate resonance adds bite to basslines. A high resonance in hardstyle gives sharpness; low resonance in techno keeps basslines smooth.
Kick Pitch Controls the pitch of the kick drum. A lower pitch gives a deep rumbling kick, while a higher pitch adds punch. For hardstyle, use low pitch for booming kicks. For hard techno, use mid-pitch for defined kicks. A low-pitched kick in hardstyle makes it boom; a mid-range kick in techno cuts through the mix.
Kick Tone Adjusts the overall color of the kick drum. A bright tone makes the kick sharper, while a darker tone adds depth. Use bright tone for sharp, cutting kicks in hardstyle. For hard techno, use a darker tone for weight. A bright tone in hardstyle adds aggressiveness; a darker tone in techno gives more body.
Octave Range Changes the octave in which the sound plays. A higher octave makes sounds brighter, while a lower octave deepens them. For hardstyle bass, use low octaves. For techno leads, use wider octave ranges. A low octave on a hardstyle bass gives depth; a higher octave for a sharp techno lead.
Oscillator Mix Adjusts the balance between different waveforms used to create the sound. Mixing oscillators creates fuller, richer sounds. For hardstyle, mix sawtooth and square waves for full sounds. For techno, mix sine and square waves for depth. A hardstyle lead mixes saw and square waves for fullness; a techno bassline mixes sine and square for depth.
Pitch Decay Controls how much the pitch drops after the sound starts. A quick decay gives a sharp drop; a slower decay is more subtle. Quick pitch decay for hardstyle kicks. For hard techno, subtle pitch decay keeps tightness. A fast decay on a hardstyle kick adds punch; a slower decay on a lead in techno adds tension.
Punch Emphasizes the initial hit of the sound. Added punch makes sounds sharper and more aggressive. Use punch for hardstyle kicks and aggressive leads. For techno, use punch on drums and basslines. A punchy hardstyle kick hits immediately; a punchy techno snare feels tight.
Release Time Controls how long the sound continues after the key is released. A short release cuts the sound off sharply; a long release makes it fade out. Short release for tight sounds in hardstyle. Long release for atmospheric pads in hard techno. A quick release on a hardstyle kick stops it abruptly; a long release on a pad in techno creates smoothness.
Reverse Amount Adds a reverse effect to the sound. Creates a swishy, reversed sound before the main attack. Use reverse for build-ups in hardstyle. For hard techno, use it for unique percussive effects. A reverse effect in a hardstyle build-up adds tension; reverse percussion adds flair in techno.
